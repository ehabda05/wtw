This is a story about friendship. Friendship runs on time together, and that time is collapsing: Americans aged 15 to 24 spent 153 minutes a day with friends in person in 2003, and 67 in 2019, before COVID. You've lived the reason. Everyone hearts the idea, and six days later, nothing. That's the gap Homi fills.

Why does it keep happening? One person always organizes: a study of students' phone logs found one side is almost twice as likely to reach out. Silence feels like rejection, so nobody chases. Ask everyone at once and fewer answer: 36 percent alone versus 14 in a crowd. And every planning tool pulls you out of the chat.

Privacy by design

Ask it what it knows. Tell it to forget.

  • It stores the plan, never the conversation: no table can hold message text.
  • Everything personal is encrypted at rest, and logs scrub names and numbers.
  • It never asks where you are: one typed neighborhood, kept with the plan.
  • It deletes everything 24 hours after the event, or now with homi forget.
  • DMs and calls only reach people who opted in, and the group is always told.
7 / 9
Privacy is the product, not a footnote. Homi stores the plan, never the conversation, and it's encrypted. It never asks where you are. It deletes everything a day after the event. Anyone can ask what it knows and get the full dump, or say forget and it's gone. Private DMs and calls only happen for people who opted in. The album, the tab and the trip stay only until the group deletes them, and "homi stop" deletes everything.
How it works

Thin adapters, a pure engine, and a store that forgets.

8 / 9
Under the hood: friends talk to Homi in their existing chats through Photon. Thin adapters turn each message into a typed event, so raw text never leaves memory. A pure state machine decides what happens, which makes every rule testable. Places get only a derived coordinate, the call gets only the question, and storage is encrypted and swept.
